## About

I am an M.S. student in Biostatistics at Yale School of Public Health, with an undergraduate background in Computer Science and Statistics from UC Berkeley.

My research interests lie at the intersection of statistical methodology, computational biology, and biomedical data science. I am particularly interested in developing and evaluating statistical and computational approaches for complex, high-dimensional biological data.

## Current Research

### Generative Modeling for Spatial Multi-omics

I am currently working with Professor Qiao Liu at Yale on research involving paired spatial transcriptomic and chromatin accessibility data. My work includes evaluating multimodal representations, reproducing existing computational methods, and investigating generative approaches to modeling cross-modal biological structure.

*Ongoing research.*

### Global Geography of ADRD Clinical Trials

I am also studying geographic patterns in Alzheimer's disease and related dementias (ADRD) clinical trials, examining how trial-site participation relates to disease burden and national development. This work aims to better characterize geographic representation and disparities in global clinical research.

*Ongoing research.*

## Research Interests

- Statistical inference and Bayesian modeling
- Generative modeling and high-dimensional statistical learning
- Single-cell and spatial multi-omics
- Global health, epidemiology, and health equity
